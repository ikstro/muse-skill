# Operate: a working surface

Steps 1 to 5 when the fence's mode is Operate, and the Operate half of a hybrid. On a working surface the visitor finishes a task; a direction is a behaviour, not a picture. Three findings from the record shape this file. Three stills per card made the directions indistinguishable (a storefront, 2026-10-30: "visually and in meaning I cannot tell how they differ"); the owner asked for one prototype where each idea's killer feature is a switch. The persuade jury scored the three behaviours that later became required at 12 to 14 of 25, each with "any site could copy it"; a persuade rubric punishes exactly what wins here. And a jury with a plain "difference" criterion crowned a costume (a search form dressed as a boarding pass, 2026-10-05) that the owner banned three weeks later.

## 1. Idea types (behaviours)

Every entry names its type. At least six types in the list, at most four per type. No metaphor world on an operate surface: a costume is a penalty, not a type.

- **where the answer opens**: what a tap reveals and where: in place, attached to the field, as a row that grows, never a page away.
- **what it does unasked**: prefill, remember, suggest, compute before being asked (the week of prices before the calendar; the last search already in the form).
- **what it refuses**: the rule that makes a wrong state impossible (a task cannot be saved without an owner; the same city twice; a paid extra never preselected).
- **what stays visible**: what never leaves the screen through the whole task (the trip, the running total, the return slot that becomes "+ add return" instead of vanishing).
- **what it counts for you**: a figure the user would otherwise compute in their head (the total for both, the difference over the cheapest, "every day this week costs the same").
- **what can be undone**: the way back from any step; what is kept when the user changes their mind.
- **the empty and the wrong**: no results, no flights, the error, shown in place in the surface's own type, with the fix one tap away.
- **the handoff**: how one step becomes the next (the fields travel up and become the header; the chosen row rides into the summary).
- **one gesture**: the single authored interaction (drag, swap, hold, flip) and what it changes on screen.
- **the first three seconds** (hybrid only): what the surface promises before the first tap, when it also has to sell.

## 2. Entry shape

```
N. [type] «Name» — Picture: the state on screen, at 390 and at the desktop width — Moment: before (the state) / during (the tap or drag and what moves) / after (what the user now knows or has), with durations — Want: what the user mid-task says, in their words — Proof: the truth or rule it uses — Asset: U product UI in HTML · D a recording that must exist · R a rule the owner must confirm — Joins: audience line + truth
```

The Moment is a gesture in three beats; "a calmer form" is a mood, "the chosen flight rides up into the header and the return list arrives mirrored" is a moment. The Want is said mid-task, after the gesture: "I never lost my flight while I picked the fare".

Data honesty: every figure on screen comes from a recording or is computed from recorded figures. An entry that needs a recording that does not exist is tagged D and the recording is asked for; it is never faked and the entry is never dropped for it (the record has four entries dropped for invented figures on one run, and the two-adult week recorded the next day).

Entry zero is the incumbent (the current screen or the legacy flow) in the same shape. Seeds, taste tags and the owner's picture as in [ideation.md](ideation.md).

## 3. Jury

Procedure as in ideation.md: a fresh opus agent, the shuffled list without tags, the fence, the anchors from `record.md` (same mode), two readings, "what is missing". The rubric is this one.

1. As the user from the AUDIENCE block, in the middle of the task:
   - **DONE** 1–5, doubled: after this, the task ends sooner or with fewer decisions. The jury writes the sentence in the user's words. 1 means "nothing changed for me"; 5 means "that was the step I dreaded, and it is gone".
   - **CLEAR** 1–5: within three seconds they know where they are, what just changed and what to do next.
   - **TRUST** 1–5: nothing surprises them badly: no figure they cannot explain, nothing lost silently, nothing destructive without a way back, nothing they need hidden.
2. As the product design lead:
   - **OWN** 1–5: only for a behaviour a rival cannot ship without this product's data or rule. A different look, a different layout or a different decoration scores 0 here, with one exception: when the fence lists the product's own world or material as a free axis (a game, a toy, a product whose world is what it sells), a look made of that material scores as a behaviour would; a look borrowed from elsewhere still scores 0. The replay on record: a game HUD whose controls were made of the world's own bricks was the owner's pick and scored 15 under the plain rule.
   - **PENALTY** 0–2: novelty for its own sake: a gesture that replaces a standard control, a costume, a step the user did not ask for, a metaphor.
   - **FIT** yes/no: made of the product's own components and the platform's habits; works in every theme and language the fence names. A control pattern from another platform fails it: a bottom sheet in a desktop window, a hover-only reveal on touch, a swipe on a pointer surface. The replay on record: a desktop tool's entry with a bottom sheet was featured at 18 and the owner rejected it for the sheet.
   - **RULES** yes/no: passes every hard rule in the fence (one primary button per step, the total visible before payment, nothing paid preselected, declining as visible as accepting, and whatever else the product has decided). "One primary button per step" means one on a step that asks for an action and none on a list that only shows; the check asserts it that way.

Total = 2×DONE + CLEAR + TRUST + OWN − PENALTY (maximum 25). An entry that fails FIT or RULES is listed and cannot be featured. Ties for the last featured place break by dice; the loser goes to `record.md` as unpicked when it scored 18 or more. The fourth place moves by one or two points between two juries of the same list; a pick that lands fifth is not a rubric error.

On a hybrid, the entries of type "the first three seconds" get a second line scored with the persuade rubric from ideation.md (WANT doubled, CLEAR, TALK, OWN); the card shows both totals, and the featured set holds at least one of them, unless the fence locks the first screen's composition; then the orchestrator waives the slot and says so on the board (the dry run on record forced a 15 into the four over three entries at 17).

An entry that fails RULES by one removable part (it drops a locked element, adds one field) is listed as **fixable** with the one change named; the orchestrator may put the fixed entry on the board tagged `(combined)` from the entry and the lock. The dry run on record lost a 20 and an 18 to this before the rule existed.

**What is missing** is required, with two lines added to ideation.md's: a state no entry handles (empty, error, loading, a change of mind), and a rule from the fence that no entry makes visible to the user.

Featured = the top three or four by total, no two of the same type, plus the owner's picture. On an operate surface the featured entries are not rivals for one slot: most of them will coexist as features of one screen. The board says so.

## 4. The idea board on operate

As in ideation.md: every entry that passed FIT and RULES gets one frame of its state at the desktop width; featured entries get three frames and a 390 frame. The frames are plans of the state, on the project's tokens, with real recorded data. The board adds one line under the fence: "On this surface the featured directions become features of one prototype; pick the ones to build as switches, promote any other, and name any that must be always on."

The owner picks; the owner may also mark an entry **always** (a rule, not a switch) or **never**.

## 5. After the jury: features and one prototype, not comps

A behaviour cannot be judged from a still, and separate comps hide how behaviours combine. So the picks become a feature list and one prototype with switches.

**`features.json`** in the lab folder, one object per picked or promoted entry: `id` (group.name), `group`, `title` in the owner's language, `what` in one line, `kind` (`toggle` or `choice`), `off` and `on` (or `options`, each with an id and a line), `start` (featured entries on, the rest off; for a choice, the jury's best variant), `idea` (the board number), `data` (which recording it reads), `moment` (true for the one authored motion), `dependsOn`. Three more lists: `always` (the rules and the states the owner marked always; they are not switches), `controls` (theme, skin, data set or party, route, a long-strings language check when the fence names languages), `dropped` (entries not built, each with its reason in one line; they also go to `record.md`).

**The prototype** (`prototype/index.html`, `app.js`, `app.css`, `data.js` generated from the recordings by a small script; plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript, no framework, no build, opens from `file://`):

- the task's flow on the recorded data, every step the fence names, ending where the fence says it ends;
- a **panel** in the owner's language: on desktop it floats at the end edge and collapses to a tab; on a phone it opens from a button as a sheet over the content; it never pushes the page. One row per feature: the title, the board number, the one-line "what it does", the switch or the choice. Tapping the title replays the feature's moment from the start (reveal on tap, never on hover). Each row carries three verdict chips in the owner's words (for example всем / опция / убрать) and the panel has one button that copies the verdicts as one line for the chat (`features: a=всем; b=опция; c=убрать`). A second button, "from the start", restarts the flow;
- the panel's state (switches, choices, controls, the current step) lives in the address hash, so a link reproduces a combination; the starting state is each feature's `start`;
- one neutral look from the project's tokens, the same for every combination. The look is decided elsewhere and is never a switch; the fence's screen rules are never switches either;
- `prefers-reduced-motion`: no movement, state changes at once.

**The check** (`prototype/check.mjs`, node, the project's Playwright): walks the flow with the start state, then with each switch flipped on its own and each choice option on its own, at 390 and the desktop width, in each skin; asserts the fence's RULES on every step (one primary button, the total visible when the fence says so, every figure in the set allowed by the recordings); writes `prototype/shots/<feature>-<state>-<width>.png` for the step where the feature shows; exits non-zero on any failure. The builder runs it until it passes and looks at the shots before reporting.

Builder: one opus agent (effort high) for the first pass, briefed with the fence verbatim, `features.json`, the data rules, the panel spec above and the check; report under ten lines (paths, what plays, one risk, any recording it needed and did not have). Later passes add feature groups; each pass is one brief.

**The owner's reaction** comes back as verdicts from the panel plus words. A feature marked for all goes to the brief as the design; an option goes to the brief as a setting (per tenant, per profile, per user, as the product has it); a removal goes to `record.md` with the owner's words. Then the brief as in ideation.md, with one more block: **Features**, the list with its verdicts.

## 6. Critic on operate

As in [critic.md](critic.md), with these substitutions: the critic receives, per feature, the recorded moment as a frame strip and the state shots (empty, mid-task, error), not one contact sheet of a hero; the fence's RULES under "These are decided"; the Operate tells from [finish.md](finish.md). The studio question becomes: "how would the best working tool in this category execute this exact flow inside these decisions", and the score measures distance from that. The critic judges the task first (can the user finish; where would they hesitate; what surprises them) and the look second.

## 7. Finish on operate

The restraint pass runs per state: in each state every control has a job in that state, and what is not needed there is absent, not disabled. Then the tells check with the Operate block, the project's checks, and the handoff as in finish.md, with the prototype link and the verdict line as the record of what was decided.
