Locks

  • Design system: Figtree only; type scale, colours, buttons, nav, panels and task rows as in the Lanework DS (owner)
  • Backgrounds: white or surface #f4f4f1 (owner)
  • Action: "Start free" (accent button) with the price line "$6 per person / month · free for 3" near it (owner)
  • Copy in English (owner)
  • Facts only from the product sheet (T1–T6); no invented customers, logos or statistics (owner)
  • Product UI shown as real, working HTML, never a screenshot in a browser frame (owner)

Principles

  • One product as the protagonist: the list of who owns what.
  • One promise per viewport.
  • Calm, not busy: one thing moves at a time.
  • Show the task, not the dashboard.
  • Every name, date and number comes from the same mock world.

Audience

  • Who: the lead of a small studio or product team, 6 people, who runs the work and also does it.
  • Moment: Monday, 9:40. The standup is in its 25th minute and nobody can say who owns the client's invoice fix.
  • Uses today: a shared spreadsheet nobody updates, plus a chat thread as the to-do list.
  • Would show a friend: the Friday summary that wrote itself and went straight to the client.
  • Stops them buying: "another tool everyone has to keep updated"; "migrating our sheet will take a week".

Product truths

  • T1 Every task has exactly one owner and one due date; no unassigned tasks
  • T2 The Friday summary writes itself from what moved; one click sends it
  • T3 Quiet hours: notifications only inside each person's working hours
  • T4 Paste a spreadsheet, get a board, in one paste
  • T5 $6 per person per month; free for up to 3
  • T6 The Monday view: who owns what this week, readable in about 30 seconds

Free axes

  • Promise / H1 · Product presentation · Moment · Composition and rhythm · Proof · Authored content · Typographic delivery · World / metaphor

Clichés: a product screenshot in a browser frame, floating and tilted over a gradient blob · "All your work in one place" and kin · three feature cards with icon, heading, two lines · a logo wall · the predictable opposite (black terminal page, monospace everything, "ship faster" bravado).

Selling mechanics: the real product, big and early · one promise · the price next to the action · the product doing its one job in front of you.

    What is missing

    • Truth no entry uses well: T5. Only I touches price, and it fails; nobody makes "free for 3" the try-tonight hook next to the action. T3 has a single entry (L), and it loses the product.
    • Fear no entry answers with proof: "another tool everyone has to keep updated." No entry shows the other five people's upkeep being near zero. R proves the report stays true, not that the team won't resent feeding it.
    • Moment no entry shows: the chain from setup to payoff: paste the sheet Sunday night (T4), read the Monday view at 9:40 (T6). Also absent: the teammate's side of the same Monday, and T6's "about 30 seconds" shown as a real, timed read of real UI (B tried it with bars).
