### Notes behind the scores

- **Figures checked against data.json.** EU's deltas +39.20 and +98.55 are per adult (169.06 and 228.41 over 129.86) and correct. EX's +78.38 is for two (338.11 − 259.73) and correct. EJ's 517.15 = 259.73 + 257.42 (two adults, both ways, Base) is correct. EY's "every day 30 Nov – 6 Dec costs the same: 259.73" matches the recorded two-adult calendar.
- **EG** shows "129.86 per adult × 2 = 259.73", which does not add up (129.86 × 2 = 259.72). The two-adult total is its own recorded fare, not a product of two prices. The entry that exists to prove figures shows a sum the passenger can catch. Show the source instead (recorded fare for 2 adults, Base, USD, one way) and drop the multiplication. TRUST 3 for that; OWN 3 because "every figure has a source one tap away" is this product's rule and a locked acceptance item.
- **EJ** answers the price but not "with a bag": "from 517.15" is Base, with no checked bag. The sentence needs "without a checked bag" or a second figure. The count-up of a price is decoration (PEN 1).
- **EX** shows "0.00" before any choice, and a zero total reads as broken. The Want ("before I picked") needs the from-total (517.15 for two) there instead.
- **EU, EL** state their deltas per adult on a trip for two. For this passenger the delta should be for both (+78.38), or say "per adult".
- **EP** draws only the marks that change, so a later brand read alone looks like it lacks the bag. Keep every mark (the owner rejects greying out, so dim nothing) and emphasise the change.
- **EK** is a setting; the price for two should simply be the default.
- **EW** RULES no: it inserts a question screen the page decides (the step order belongs to the server recipe), and the first results view shows no trip (principle 1).
- **ER** FIT no: translucent sheets are not the product's components, and a metaphor world is barred on an operate surface.
- **ES** RULES no: the bundle page takes the flight out of view (principle 1), and the legacy flow carries an upgrade dialog, so declining is not as visible as accepting.
- **ED** is real, and only this one-engine product can do it, but it is a demonstration for tenants, not something a passenger does.
- **Persuade lines** (the search screen before the first tap): EC 13, EV 15, ES 5. Neither candidate is a strong first screen. EV holds more to want (the week of prices before the calendar); EC holds more to trust.

## Featured

1. **EU «The row becomes the fare»** (transformation · where the answer opens · 21). It removes the step the passenger dreads, the full-page bundle table. The fare choice opens from the row that was tapped, the times stay as the header, and the deltas sit over Base. It is exactly the owner's "the row grows into the page", and the opposite of the rejected bottom sheet.
2. **EL «No nagging»** (enemy / tension · what it refuses · 19). This is the persona's friend sentence made into a rule. Base is confirmed like any fare, with no dialog, and the bag note lives in the summary. It makes "declining as visible as accepting" visible.
3. **EM «The trip rail»** (moment of use · what stays visible · 19). The trip and the total for both never leave the screen, which keeps the first principle on every step. The chosen card travels into the rail (carry-over motion), and on a phone the rail becomes the bottom bar.
4. **EC «Search opens the route»** (ritual / reveal · the handoff · 17, persuade 13). It is the hybrid's first-screen entry and the roadmap lock ("Search plays the transition into the results"): From and To ride up and become the results header. It is taken over EV (persuade 15, Operate 16) because Operate decides and because it carries the owner's carry-over taste. EF (17, the same type) is the same handoff one step later and joins it as a feature, not a rival.

Runners-up: E2 (18, copy-led), a natural partner to EU inside the grown row; EN (18), the same behaviour as EM; EF, EH and EX (17). As operate.md says, the featured four are not rivals for one slot: EC → EU → EL → EM is one screen sequence, with EF, E2 and EH as features inside it.

## What is missing

- **Over-represented:** drawings of the fare comparison (EE, EP, ER, E2, EQ) and drawings of time (EZ, EQ, ET). The running total appears three times as one behaviour (EM, EN, EX). The search screen itself, the first thing a visitor meets, has only two real entries (EC, EV), and neither is strong as persuasion.
- **Missing types:** no entry is **what can be undone**. Only EL is **what it refuses**, and only EV touches **what it does unasked** (the week strip). Nothing prefills the last search, puts recents and favourites after the button, or opens the calendar with prices already on it.
- **A truth no entry uses:**
  - Seats left per brand, recorded on every brand and named as a selling mechanic in the fence.
  - EUR as its own fares rather than a conversion (115.97).
  - The family recording (1 adult, 1 child, 1 infant), whose week has no prices at all.
  - One engine, many airlines: only ED uses it, and not for the passenger.
- **A fear no entry answers with proof:** "the price changing at the end" is answered only by EG, whose breakdown does not add up. "Extras that appear late" has no answer: no entry shows on the results which extras are paid later (seat, meal "for charge") and that their price is not yet known.
- **A state no entry handles (operate):**
  - A change of mind: the passenger goes back after choosing the outbound or the fare. Nothing says what is kept, where they land, or whether the return choice survives.
  - Loading on real V0: EB stages a delay that V0 does not have.
  - A missing field on the search form (every missing field at once, the message under its field): no entry draws it.
  - The family search with an empty week.
- **A fence rule no entry makes visible (operate):** no entry shows "switching language keeps progress": no one switches language mid-choice, with Kazakh strings about 30 % longer, and keeps the trip. "One primary button per step" and "nothing paid preselected" are implied but never shown as a state. No entry answers `prefers-reduced-motion`, though half the list is motion.
- **Only this product could stage:**
  - EG, the source of every figure, once the arithmetic is fixed.
  - EB, because the two-step load is this product's rule.
  - EL, the engine's own hint without the nag.
  - ED, one engine with two skins.

  Everything else a rival airline site could ship.

  - A real loading recording, or a recorded ETS two-step response, before EB is shown as fact.
  - A two-adult per-brand delta set, to show deltas for both.
